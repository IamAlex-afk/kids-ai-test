AI lesson for high school: which AI risks are real?

Grades 9–12 (ages 14–17) · 45 min

Lesson goal

Students distinguish risks backed by cases and research from speculation about the future, and argue from evidence using five questions.

AI4K12 big idea: societal impact. UNESCO: ethics of AI and a human-centred mindset, "apply" level (our estimate).

What you need

  • A screen.
  • Eight statement cards per group: 1) Face recognition systems make more errors for some groups of people than for others. 2) AI makes it possible to mass-produce unreliable content. 3) There are disputes about copyright in the texts and images AI was trained on. 4) In some jobs, AI already replaces part of the work. 5) AI can pursue a "similar" goal instead of the one people intended. 6) AI capabilities are growing faster than safety research. 7) "AI will kill us all." 8) "AI has no risks at all."
  • A "Risk map" sheet with two axes: "Is there evidence today?" and "How serious is it?".

Lesson on this site: "The landscape of AI risk: near-term vs speculative" →

Lesson flow (45 minutes)

  1. 5 min. Warm-up. Anonymous sticky notes: "The biggest risk of AI is…". Read out 3–4.
  2. 7 min. Site lesson. Read it and answer "Check yourself". Explain the lesson's terms: bias, misalignment, proxy goal.
  3. 12 min. Risk map. Groups of 4 place the cards on the axes and write one line for each: "why here".
  4. 8 min. Five questions from the lesson. Each group takes two cards and answers: which risks exactly? how likely? how soon? who bears them? who benefits if they are ignored?
  5. 10 min. Debate. Two groups: "Card 7 is an exaggeration" vs "Card 8 is an exaggeration". Every argument must cite a card or a source. The teacher enforces this rule rather than picking a winner.
  6. 3 min. Wrap-up. Each student completes: "A real risk right now is… because…; a speculation is…".

Discussion questions

  • Why does the news often mix the two kinds of risk?
  • Who should be responsible for risks 1–4: developers, governments or users?
  • Which of these affects you already today?

How to check understanding

A completed risk map and the wrap-up sentence. Grade the reasoning, not the "right" place of a card.

Differentiation

  • For students who need support: 4 cards — 1, 2, 7 and 8.
  • Early finishers find the primary source for one card and check whether it supports it.

Note for families

Today students discussed which AI risks are backed by evidence now and which are speculation about the future. Ask your teen which risk they see as the biggest and what that view is based on.

Sources

  • Buolamwini, Gebru, "Gender Shades", 2018 — card 1: error rates of up to 34.7% for one group vs 0.8% for another
  • Center for AI Safety, "Statement on AI Risk" — an example of a long-term risk
  • AI4K12 — big idea "Societal impact"
