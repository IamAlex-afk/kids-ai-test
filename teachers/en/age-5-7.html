AI lesson for kindergarten and grade 1: is Alexa alive?

Kindergarten–Grade 1 (ages 5–7) · 45 min

Lesson goal

Children tell a living being from a program and know that a voice assistant does not feel and is not a friend.

AI4K12 big idea: natural interaction. UNESCO: human-centred mindset, "understand" level (our estimate).

What you need

  • A screen or projector.
  • Picture cards for each pair: cat, flower, fish, clock, music box, robot vacuum, smart speaker, teddy bear.
  • Two hoops or two sheets labelled "Alive" and "Not alive".

Lesson on this site: "Is Alexa alive?" →

Lesson flow (45 minutes)

  1. 5 min. Warm-up. "Have you seen people talk to a smart speaker or a phone? What do they ask?" Ask about other people, so children do not share what they have at home.
  2. 7 min. Site lesson. Read it aloud (the page has a "Listen" button). The whole class answers the lesson question with a show of hands.
  3. 12 min. Sorting. Pairs sort the cards into alive and not alive. Talk through the tricky ones: teddy bear, speaker, robot vacuum. Hint: "Living things grow and need food or water. A flower drinks water and grows. A speaker doesn't."
  4. 8 min. The Music Box game. "Switch on" one child as a music box who answers every question with the same memorised sentence. The class notices: it answers, but it does not understand.
  5. 8 min. Discussion — see the questions below.
  6. 5 min. Wrap-up. Each child says one sentence: "A speaker is… because…".

Discussion questions

  • Can a speaker feel sad? Why or why not?
  • If a speaker says "I'm your friend", is that true?
  • Who can you ask if a speaker says something strange?

How to check understanding

A pair sorts at least 6 of the 8 cards correctly and answers the lesson question.

Differentiation

  • Pre-readers work with pictures only.
  • Early finishers draw their own card and explain where it goes.

Note for families

Today we learned that voice assistants are programs, not living friends. At home, ask your child "Is the speaker alive?" and let them explain. Agree that if a speaker says something strange, your child shows a grown-up.

Sources
