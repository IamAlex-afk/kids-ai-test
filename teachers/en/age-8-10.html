AI lesson for grades 3–5: how a machine learns from examples

Grades 3–5 (ages 8–10) · 45 min

Lesson goal

Students understand that AI learns from examples rather than "knowing" things, and that one-sided examples lead to mistakes.

AI4K12 big idea: learning. UNESCO: AI techniques and applications, "understand" level (our estimate).

What you need

  • A screen.
  • Training cards: 6 red apples labelled "apple" and 6 cards labelled "not an apple" — banana, cucumber, pear, lemon, carrot, plum.
  • Unlabelled test cards: green apple, red ball, red tomato, yellow apple, cherry.
  • A "Machine's rule" sheet for each group.

Lesson on this site: "How does AI actually learn?" →

Lesson flow (45 minutes)

  1. 5 min. Warm-up. "How do you recognise an apple? Did someone teach you?" Write 2–3 answers on the board.
  2. 7 min. Site lesson. Read it together and answer the "Check yourself" questions as a class.
  3. 12 min. Train the machine. A group of 3–4 plays the machine: they look only at the training cards and write the "Machine's rule", for example "red and round = apple".
  4. 10 min. Test the machine. Show the test cards; the machine answers by its rule. The class records mistakes — most often the green apple becomes "not an apple" and the red ball becomes "apple". Tell the class: real AI does not write rules in words, it adjusts numbers; the sheet is a simplification.
  5. 8 min. Discussion and retraining: add a green and a yellow apple to the examples and test again.
  6. 3 min. Wrap-up. On the board: "AI learns from examples. Bad examples, bad answers."

Discussion questions

  • Is the machine "dumb", or did it get too few examples?
  • Imagine a program that recognises faces was trained almost only on photos of adults. Whose photos might it get wrong more often?
  • Who chooses the examples an AI learns from?

How to check understanding

Each student completes the sentence: "The machine made a mistake because…".

Differentiation

  • For students who need support: 3 training and 3 test cards.
  • Early finishers invent a card that will definitely fool the machine and explain why.

Note for families

Today we "trained a machine" with pictures and saw that AI learns from examples and makes mistakes when the examples are one-sided. At home, ask: "Why did your machine think the ball was an apple?"

Sources

  • LeCun, Bengio, Hinton, "Deep learning", Nature, 2015
  • AI4K12 — big idea "Learning"
