Free AI lesson plans for teachers, K–12

Four ready-to-teach 45-minute lessons for K–1, grades 3–5, 6–8 and 9–12. Each one is built around a short lesson on this site and an unplugged activity.

Students do not need accounts or a chatbot: one screen at the front of the room is enough.

Choose an age group

  • Kindergarten–Grade 1 (ages 5–7) — "Is Alexa alive?" Telling living things from programs.
  • Grades 3–5 (ages 8–10) — "How does a machine learn?" The class trains a "machine" with picture cards.
  • Grades 6–8 (ages 11–13) — "AI guesses the next word." Why AI sounds confident but gets things wrong.
  • Grades 9–12 (ages 14–17) — "Which AI risks are real?" A risk map and an evidence-based debate.

What every plan includes

  • A lesson goal and the big idea about AI it teaches.
  • What you need: a lesson on this site and paper cards you can make yourself.
  • A minute-by-minute flow: warm-up, site lesson, unplugged activity, discussion, wrap-up.
  • Discussion questions, a quick check and options for different learners.
  • A three-line note for families to continue the conversation at home.

What the plans are based on

  • AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas in AI": perception; representation and reasoning; learning; natural interaction; societal impact. ai4k12.org
  • UNESCO AI competency framework for students (2024): four dimensions — a human-centred mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, AI system design — and three levels: understand, apply, create. Which level fits which plan is our own estimate. unesco.org

Why no chatbots

You can teach how AI works without them: students need no accounts, and this site asks for no sign-up and does not store answers.

How to print

Press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac; on a phone, Share → Print) and choose "Save as PDF" or a printer. The site menu and buttons are left out of the printout.

How this was made: the author wrote these materials with the help of AI tools; claims about AI were checked against the open primary sources linked on this page. Teachers and students have not reviewed them yet.

Independent free material. Not an official curriculum document of any education authority. Teachers decide how to use it.
