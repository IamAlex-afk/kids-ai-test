What is AI — in simple words

Artificial intelligence (AI) is software that learns from a great many examples and uses them to guess an answer: what is in a picture, which word comes next, which video to show. It is useful, but it is not alive and it can be wrong.

What is known

  • Modern AI is not given ready-made rules: it finds patterns in large sets of data by itself and adjusts its internal settings. [1]
  • Recognising a cat in a photo or a word in speech is "perception": getting meaning out of signals. It is one of the five big ideas of AI, together with representation and reasoning, learning, interaction with people, and impact on society. [2]
  • Programs that write text can "hallucinate" — produce something wrong just as smoothly as something right. [3]
  • In the European Union the law requires that people are told when they are talking to an AI, and that fakes made by AI (deepfakes) are labelled. [4]
  • In an MIT study, children aged 3–10 who played with voice assistants talked about how smart the assistants were and "who" they were. [5]

By age

Ages 5–7

For ages 5–7: an adult reads together with the child.

How to say it

  • AI is a guessing program. It was shown lots and lots of cat pictures — and now it can recognise a cat.
  • It is not alive: it is not happy, not sad, and it is not your friend.
  • It can get things wrong. If you are not sure, ask a grown-up.

Ages 8–10

How to say it

  • AI learns from examples, like you do in practice — only it has millions of them.
  • It looks for patterns, but it does not understand the world the way you do.
  • An AI answer is a guess. Check important things in a book or with an adult.

Ages 11–13

How to say it

  • AI is a model: from data it picks the most likely answer.
  • That is why it can be wrong and sound sure — this is called a hallucination.
  • Check: who else confirms it? Is there a primary source?

Ages 14+

How to say it

  • AI is a statistical model trained on huge sets of data; how it behaves depends on the data and on how people set it up.
  • A likely answer is not necessarily a true one: errors, skewed data and fakes are real.
  • There are rules: in the EU a chatbot must say it is an AI, and deepfakes must be labelled. Ask who made the system and what it does with your data.

Our conclusion

AI is a strong tool for guessing from examples, but it is not a friend and it does not know everything. The key skill next to it is to check the answer and decide for yourself.
