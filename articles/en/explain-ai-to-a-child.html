How to explain AI to a child

Your child already meets AI: a voice assistant, suggestions on a phone, a video feed. You can explain it in simple words — and a small hands-on try works better than a lecture.

What is known

  • Children aged 3–10 who played with voice assistants talked about how smart they were and "who" they were (MIT study). [1]
  • In its 2025 guidance, UNICEF requires AI for children to ensure safety, protect children's data and privacy, be transparent, and prepare children for life with AI. [2]
  • UNESCO's AI competency framework for students (2024) puts a "human-centred mindset" first: understanding and asserting your own role in relation to AI. [3]
  • Text from an AI can be wrong even when it sounds confident — researchers call this hallucination. [4]
  • In the EU a chatbot must tell you that you are talking to an AI, and deepfakes must be labelled. [5]

By age

Ages 5–7

For ages 5–7: an adult reads together with the child.

How to say it

  • "It is a program. It learned lots of examples and it guesses."
  • "It is not alive and not a friend, even if it talks nicely."
  • "We don't tell it our name, address or photos."

Ages 8–10

How to say it

  • "AI learns from examples. If the examples are bad, it gets things wrong."
  • "Its answer is a guess. Let's check it together."
  • "If an AI asks something about you, ask me first."

Ages 11–13

How to say it

  • "AI picks a likely answer; it does not know the truth."
  • "A confident tone is not proof. Look for who else confirms it."
  • "You can talk your homework over with AI, but the thinking and writing are yours."

Ages 14+

How to say it

  • "Who made this system, what was it trained on, and where does your data go?"
  • "A deepfake can look real — check the source."
  • "AI is a tool. Decisions and responsibility stay with people."

Common misconceptions

Myth AI is alive and can be a friend.
In fact It is a program. In the MIT study children talked about "who" the voice assistant is — so it is worth saying plainly that AI does not feel and is not a friend. [1]
Myth AI is always right because it sounds sure.
In fact AI can state wrong things confidently. Check important things against a second source. [4]
Myth You can tell AI any secret.
In fact Protecting children's data is one of UNICEF's requirements. Personal things (name, address, photos, passwords) are not for a chatbot. [2]
Myth Better to ban AI until the child grows up.
In fact UNICEF asks to prepare children for life with AI, and UNESCO to teach them to understand and assert their own role next to it. A ban does not teach that; a guided first contact does. [2][3]

Our conclusion

Explain briefly and through experience: "it is a program, it guesses from examples and can be wrong". Agree on two rules — check important things and don't share personal ones. At 5–7, only together with an adult.
