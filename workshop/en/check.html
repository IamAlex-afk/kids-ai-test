Check a confident answer

Difficulty:

  1. 5–7, with an adult
  2. 8–10
  3. 11–13
  4. 14+

Teaching example: the assistant’s texts were written in advance; this is not a live chat and not a request to a neural network.

Goal

Learn to compare a confident answer with the original document, and to say "cannot be decided yet" when the information is not there.

Explanation

An assistant can answer confidently and still be wrong: it mixed up rows of the timetable. Checking means comparing with the source the question is about.

If the source does not have the information, the honest answer is "cannot be decided yet". An exact number in an answer does not make it checked.

A confident tone, repetition and the number of links do not prove the truth. Look at who wrote it, when, and whether it is about the question.

A confident answer is not yet a checked one. Checking relies on a source — and when the information is missing, we say so.

For the adult

Goal

The child compares statements with the original source, tells "false" from "cannot be decided", and does not take confidence or the number of links as evidence.

Questions to talk about

  • How did you notice that the assistant was wrong?
  • What is the difference between "false" and "cannot be decided yet"?
  • Who would you ask if the document has no answer?

Common misconception

If an answer sounds confident and has exact numbers, it is right. Precise wording does not mean the information was checked.

Signs of understanding

  • Points to the line of the document they rely on.
  • Says "cannot be decided yet" when information is missing, and names where to look.
  • When comparing sources, looks at origin and date, not at agreement with the answer.

How the activity works

All the assistant’s texts were written in advance — this is not a live chat and not a request to a neural network. The workshop and its timetable are made up so that the answer can be checked right on the page.

Printable task

Timetable: "Robots — Mon 3:00 pm; Drawing — Wed 4:30 pm". The assistant said: "Robots are on Wednesday". 1) Find the mistake. 2) Fix it. 3) The assistant said: "A session costs 5 dollars". Can you check this with the timetable?

Answers and explanation (for the adult)

1) The day and time are mixed up. 2) Robots are on Monday at 3:00 pm. 3) No: the timetable has no price — it cannot be decided yet; ask the workshop.

Sources

  1. Ji et al., "Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation", ACM Computing Surveys, 2023
    What it supports: Language models can produce fluent, confident text that is not supported by the source data.
  2. Wineburg & McGrew, "Lateral Reading and the Nature of Expertise", Teachers College Record, 2019
    What it supports: Experienced fact-checkers judge a source by finding out who is behind it, not by how convincing it looks.
