What is OK to share?

Difficulty:

  1. 5–7, with an adult
  2. 8–10
  3. 11–13
  4. 14+

Teaching example: the assistant’s texts were written in advance; this is not a live chat and not a request to a neural network.

Goal

Learn to keep only what the task needs in a request, and not to share personal details.

Explanation

For most tasks an assistant does not need your name, address, school, phone number or password. The task can be described without them.

Sometimes details are needed for the result (for example, the address on an invitation), but you do not have to give them to the assistant: leave a gap and fill it in yourself.

Details that look harmless one by one can, together, show who you are and where to find you. If you are not sure, ask a trusted adult.

Before you send a request, ask yourself: what here does the task really need? Everything else can be removed or replaced.

For the adult

Goal

The child separates the task from personal details, removes or replaces the extras, and understands that a combination of small details can reveal who someone is.

Questions to talk about

  • What in Tim’s message is not needed to think of a name?
  • Why do the guests need the address but the assistant does not?
  • What will you do if you are not sure whether you can write something?

Common misconception

If you do not give your name, you share nothing personal. School, timetable, places and photos together can point to a person even without a name.

Signs of understanding

  • Removes the name, school, phone number and password, but keeps the point of the task.
  • Suggests a replacement: [address], "my town" instead of the street.
  • Says they would ask an adult when unsure.

How the activity works

All names, addresses and numbers are made up. The child types nothing about themselves: they only mark pieces of a ready-made text. The check compares the marks with a breakdown written in advance and explains every piece.

Printable task

Underline what is not needed: "Hi, I'm Lucy Hart from class 3B at Hill School. My password is 1234. Help me make up a riddle about a cat." Rewrite the request so that only the task is left.

Answers and explanation (for the adult)

Not needed: first and last name, class and school, password. Safe request: "Help me make up a riddle about a cat."

Sources

  1. UNICEF Innocenti, Policy guidance on AI for children
    What it supports: Protecting children’s data and privacy is one of the requirements for AI systems that children interact with.
  2. Sweeney, "Simple Demographics Often Identify People Uniquely", Carnegie Mellon University, 2000
    What it supports: A combination of a few ordinary details (ZIP code, sex, date of birth) was enough to single out most people in the data studied.
