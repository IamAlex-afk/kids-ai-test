Lesson 1 of 8

🎲 AI doesn't know the truth — it guesses the next word

When you type into ChatGPT, it doesn't look up an answer — it guesses the most likely next word, over and over, based on patterns from millions of texts it read before. It's like the autocomplete on your phone, but much more powerful. That's why it can sound totally sure and still be wrong.

Example

📱 Your phone suggests the next word when you text. AI chatbots do the same thing — just trained on far more text. Neither one actually "knows" what's true.

Check yourself

Why can AI sound 100% confident and still be wrong?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: It predicts likely words, not true ones 🎲

AI has no way to check if something is true — it only knows what usually comes next in similar text. Confident does not mean correct.

Source: Ji et al., "Survey of Hallucination in NLG", 2023

Lesson 2 of 8

🎭 Spotting deepfakes and AI-made fakes

AI can now create fake photos, videos, and even voices of real people saying things they never said. These are called "deepfakes." Some are obvious, but the best ones can fool almost anyone at first glance.

Example

🔍 Look for: hands with too many fingers, weird blinking, lighting that doesn't match, or a voice that sounds slightly "off." When in doubt, check if a trusted news source is reporting the same thing.

Check yourself

You see a shocking video of a celebrity saying something crazy. What should you do first?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: Check if real news sites are reporting it too 🔎

Video and audio can be faked by AI now. Always check if a trustworthy source confirms it before you believe or share it.

Source: Common Sense Media, "Teens, Trust, and Technology in the Age of AI", 2025

Lesson 3 of 8

📱 Why your feed shows you THAT video

YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram use AI to pick what shows up next — and it's not trying to inform you, it's trying to keep you watching as long as possible. The AI learns exactly what makes YOU personally keep scrolling.

Example

🎣 It works like a fishing hook, not a teacher. Every video it recommends was picked because people like you tend to watch "just one more."

Check yourself

What is a recommendation algorithm mainly optimized to do?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: Keep you watching as long as possible ⏳

Recommendation systems are built to maximize watch time — that's how apps make money from ads. Noticing this helps you stay in control of your own time.

Source: Covington et al., "Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Recommendations", Google, 2016

Lesson 4 of 8

🔒 What happens after you chat with an AI?

When you type something into a chatbot, it doesn't just disappear. Companies often save your conversations to improve their AI — and sometimes real people read chats to check the AI's answers.

Example

✉️ Treat an AI chat a bit like a postcard, not a locked diary — assume it could be read by someone at the company. Never type your address, passwords, or a friend's private info.

Check yourself

Is it safe to tell an AI chatbot your home address so it can "help you better"?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: No — treat it like it might not stay private 🔒

Never share real personal details with a chatbot. Your conversation may be stored, reviewed, or used to train future AI.

Source: UNESCO, "AI competency framework for students", 2024

Lesson 5 of 8

⚖️ AI can be unfair without anyone meaning it to be

AI learns from real examples made by real people — and if those examples are unbalanced, the AI copies that unfairness. Some face-recognition apps, for example, have been shown to work less accurately on darker skin tones, because of what photos they were trained on.

Example

📸 If you only ever showed an AI photos of red apples, it might not recognize a green one as an apple at all. Same idea, bigger consequences, when it's about people.

Check yourself

Why can AI end up treating some groups of people unfairly?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: The examples it learned from weren't balanced or fair 📊

AI mirrors patterns in its training data. If certain groups are underrepresented or shown unfairly, the AI repeats that pattern — nobody has to program it to be unfair on purpose.

Source: Buolamwini & Gebru, "Gender Shades", MIT Media Lab, 2018

Lesson 6 of 8

🎨 AI art — who actually owns it?

AI image generators were trained on millions of real artists' works — often without their permission. Whether that is allowed is still being decided in court. And who owns an AI picture? In the US, the Copyright Office explained in 2025: what AI created entirely on its own is not protected by copyright, and a request (a prompt) alone is not enough to make you the author. What a person adds is protected. Other countries have their own rules.

Example

⚖️ Imagine a chef who learned every recipe by tasting other people's cooking without asking, then sells a "new" dish. Is it really new? That's the debate happening with AI art right now.

Check yourself

Why is AI-generated art legally complicated?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: It was trained on real artists' work, often without their permission 📊

AI learned from people's work, often without the artists' consent — courts are deciding whether that is allowed. And a picture made entirely by AI from a single prompt is not protected by copyright, according to the US Copyright Office (2025).

Source: US Copyright Office, "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence. Part 2: Copyrightability", 2025

Lesson 7 of 8

🎮 Is that even a real person? AI in games and chats

Some "players" you meet online might not be human at all. AI-powered bots can chat, play games, and even argue with you convincingly. Some apps use AI characters designed to feel like real friends — but they're built by companies with their own goals.

Example

🕵️ Just like you'd double-check a stranger's profile, it's smart to stay a little skeptical of who — or what — you're really talking to online.

Check yourself

Can you always be 100% sure the "player" you're chatting with online is a real human?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: No — it could be an AI bot 🤖

AI chatbots and game bots can be very convincing. It's smart to stay a little skeptical, especially with people you don't know in real life.

Source: Common Sense Media, "Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs: How and Why Teens Use AI Companions", 2025

Lesson 8 of 8

📝 When AI gets your homework wrong

Using AI to help with homework can be smart — but submitting AI mistakes as your own work can hurt your grade AND your learning. The safest approach: use AI to explain a concept, then write the answer yourself, in your own words.

Example

🏋️ Using AI for every answer is like having someone else lift your weights at the gym — you won't actually get stronger, even if the weight got lifted.

Check yourself

What's the smartest way to use AI for homework?

Show the answer and explanation

Correct answer: Use it to understand, then write your own answer ✍️

AI is a great study helper — but the actual thinking and writing should still be yours, so you actually learn.

Source: Bastani et al., "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning", PNAS 2025
